###The Perelman School of Medicine UPenn Proteomics pilot (UPennPilot) study
Postmortem brains from the Center for Neurodegenerative Disease Research (CNDR) at the Perelman School of Medicine of the University of Pennsylvania were provided to Emory for this study and they were derived from control subjects and participants in the Penn ADCC, PMC, Udall Center and FTD/ALS centers.  Additional cognitively normal control brains were harvested from the general Hospital of the University of Pennsylvania (HUP) autopsy service, and these HUP cases are assed for cognitive impairment by the Galvin et al AD8 instrument.

This is a pilot as part of a greater study consisting of 8 cognitively normal (control), 6 frontotemporal lobar degeneration with ubiquitin-only inclusions and mutation within the granulin gene (FTLD-U Granulin), and 10 frontotemporal lobar degeneration with ubiquitin-only inclusions and no granulin mutation (FTLD-U No Granulin). The brains were bisected and one hemisphere was cut coronally in 1-2 cm slabs and stored at -80˚C. Paraffin-embedded tissue sections from 18 regions were evaluated with a standard battery of histochemical and immunohistochemical stains to identify the different neurodegenerative and vascular pathologies [1]. The standard battery included hematoxylin and eosin, Thioflavin S, amyloid beta (nab 228, starting in 2011), α-synuclein (Syn303), phosphorylated tau (PHF-1), and phosphorylated TDP-43 (pS409/410).
