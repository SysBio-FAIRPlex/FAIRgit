### Brain Viral Profiling in the Mayo, MSBB, and ROSMAP Cohorts (VirusResilience_Mayo.MSBB.ROSMAP)

This study details an effort to use a mitochondrial variant calling specific pipeline to call variants on the mitochondrial genome based on the WGS data generated through grants U01AG046152, U01AG046170, and U01AG046139. The goal of this study is to create a uniformly processed MT variants dataset based on donors profiled through the [ROSMAP](https://adknowledgeportal.synapse.org/Explore/Studies/DetailsPage?Study=syn3219045), [MSBB](https://adknowledgeportal.synapse.org/Explore/Studies/DetailsPage?Study=syn3159438), and [MayoRNAseq](https://adknowledgeportal.synapse.org/Explore/Studies/DetailsPage?Study=syn5550404) studies.

The dysregulation of cellular bioenergetics represents a common feature across multiple neurodegenerative diseases, including Alzheimer’s disease (AD), Parkinson’s disease, and progressive supranuclear palsy [1]. Multiple lines of evidence suggest mitochondrial dysfunction in AD, including regional hypometabolism in AD indicated by FDG-PET studies [2], alteration of energy sources toward amino acids and fatty acids [3], increased oxidative stress and ROS production [4], reduced intact MT in AD brains [5], and reports of reduced MT copy number and impaired base excision repair capacity in AD [6]. Simultaneously, evidence for a potential role for microbial interactions in AD continues to accumulate [7-12], although findings are complex, and at times discordant. Additional scientific studies illuminate heterogeneous interactions between viruses and host mitochondrial function, including degradation of mtDNA, impairment of MT protein production and localization and modulation of apoptotic programs [reviewed13]. We therefore sought to perform a characterization of MT DNA variation, copy-number and heteroplasmy for inclusion as relevant variables into our ongoing studies of viral resilience in the context of AD.

**Metadata:** Individual, biospecimen, and WGS assay metadata files for samples from each cohort are available through the relevant studies linked above.

**Data formats:** This study provides data from a joint study analysis where variants were called across the three studies. This data is available in two formats:
- All variant sites merged in a single file for the study specific batches only.
- Metrics generated for each sample within each cohort.
