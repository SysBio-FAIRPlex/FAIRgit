### The Accelerating Medicines Partnership Alzheimer's Disease Diverse Cohorts Study (AMP-AD_DiverseCohorts)

**NOTICE October 17, 2025**: On August 27, 2025, Sage identified individual metadata discrepancies between brain bank source data and the metadata posted here, for a subset of donors from the Mt Sinai Brank Bank (MSBB). These discrepancies also affected the AMP-AD 1.0 MSBB study. The AD knowledge portal team, along with our NIA partners and investigators from MSBB, have been hard at work to rectify the discrepancies. The individual-level metadata has now been thoroughly vetted against contributor-confirmed data and corrected. Overall, approximately 40% of MSBB donors had one or more incorrect metadata values; variables impacted included CERAD, Braak, age, sex, APOE genotype, CDR, and PMI. As of October 17, all metadata discrepancies except self-reported race/ethnicity data have been resolved and corrected. Race/ethnicity data will be updated as soon as possible. We have posted the re-validated data as version 10 in the MSBB study and as version 21 in the Diverse Cohorts Study, and we encourage users to explore the latest information. **At this time, we are unsure of the impact of these corrections on previous statistical analyses and we strongly urge users of this data to rerun their past analyses, compare new results to former results, and report updates.**

This study is an AMP-AD cross-consortium project focusing on generation and harmonization of new datasets representing diverse cohorts which extend the discoveries of the following parent grants: U01AG046139, U01AG046170, U01AG061357, U01AG061356, U01AG061359, and R01AG067025. This study provides post-mortem whole genome sequencing and brain bulk RNA sequencing data from over 850 individuals, including 288 Black or African American individuals and 330 Hispanic individuals representing the spectrum of Alzheimer’s Disease phenotypes.

Future work will include: harmonized whole genome sequencing, bulk RNA sequencing, and single cell RNA sequencing data processed through a common bioinformatics pipeline; release of additional harmonized clinical metadata variables; and generation of mass spec proteomic and metabolomic data for these samples; and reprocessing and release of all existing single cell RNAseq samples from previous AMP-AD studies.

**Phenotype Harmonization:** Clinical variables and harmonized derived outcomes were determined for all donors by the AMP-AD Phenotype Harmonization Working Group. Please see the [Clinical Metadata Data Dictionary](https://www.synapse.org/#!Synapse:syn51757664) for descriptions of harmonized clinical variables and allowed values. The criteria used to derive the harmonized AD outcomes from these variables are available in the [Harmonized Outcomes Explainer](https://www.synapse.org/#!Synapse:syn51757663).

**Cohort descriptions:**

_Mayo Clinic:_

The Mayo Clinic's diverse cohort comprises whole transcriptome data obtained for 301 samples of the Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (DLPFC), 231 samples of the anterior Caudate Nucleus (CN), and 330 samples of the Temporal Cortex (TCX) from the superior temporal gyrus. This cohort includes 96 Non-Hispanic White subjects, 53 African American subjects, and 182 Latin American donors. The cohort includes brain donors with neuropathological diagnosis of AD, Elderly controls (CON) without neurodegenerative diseases; and a third group with others neuropathologies that do not include AD; such as vascular dementia (VaD), dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP), pathologic aging (PA), among others.

Within this cohort, AD, Controls, and other donors are from the Mayo Clinic Brain Bank (MCBB), University of Florida (UF) and Banner Sun Health Research Institute (Banner).  All donors selected from the MCBB, UF and Banner underwent neuropathologic evaluation by Dr. Dennis Dickson, Dr. Stefan Prokop, or Dr. Thomas Beach, respectively. All AD donors had definite diagnosis according to the NINCDS-ADRDA criteria and had Braak NFT stage of IV or greater. Controls donors had Braak NFT stage of III or less, CERAD neuritic and cortical plaque densities of 0 (none) or 1 (sparse) and lacked any of the following pathologic diagnoses: AD, Parkinson’s disease (PD), DLB, VaD, PSP, motor neuron disease (MND), CBD, Pick’s disease (PiD), Huntington’s disease (HD), FTLD, hippocampal sclerosis (HipScl) or dementia lacking distinctive histology (DLDH). Donors in with Other diagnoses have neuropathologic diagnoses other than AD.

The Mayo Clinic uses a quantitative approach to the evaluation of tau tangles that is used to translate to Braak stage. Using thioflavin-S allows evaluation of mature and ghost tangles that better represent the poor health of the tangle-bearing neuron. and represented in decimal increments of 0.5. When discrete numbers are required for Braak stage, these values can be rounded down. In this circumstance they represent that tangle pathology was minimally observed in the next area required for Braak staging, but insufficient to reach the next stage. The only consideration to stage is regarding the value 0.5. In such cases, values are  round up to 1 to indicate the presence of tangle pathology.
